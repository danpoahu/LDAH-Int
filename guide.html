LDAH Internal Dashboard — User Guide

Everything the dashboard does, in the order you are likely to need it.

How this guide is arranged. Tier 1 is the handful of things to know on your first day. Tier 2 is what most staff touch every day. Tier 3 is the full reference — look things up here rather than reading it through. Sections marked Admin or SuperAdmin are only visible to those roles, so if you cannot find one, that is why.
Tier 1 · Quick Start

What Is LDAH-Int

LDAH-Int is the staff dashboard. It holds the records behind everything LDAH does: the families we work with, every conversation we have with them, every event and who came, and the content on the public website.

Two ideas explain most of it. A Contact is a person — usually a parent or guardian. An Interaction is one logged conversation with that person. Almost everything else hangs off those two.

The dashboard also writes to the public site. When you change an event, an FAQ or a resource here, the website changes too. There is no separate step.

Logging In

  1. Go to the dashboard link and sign in with your LDAH Google account.
  2. Your role decides what you can see. If a section in this guide is not in your sidebar, your role does not have it.
Trouble signing in? Make sure you are signed into your @ldahawaii.org account and not a personal one. If you have several Google accounts in the same browser, sign out of all of them and sign back in with the LDAH one.

Getting Help

Three kinds of help are built in.

  • The small circled i next to a control explains that control. Every one ends with a link to the assistant if it did not answer your question.
  • The ? button at the bottom left opens the AI helper. Ask it in plain words.
  • The chat bubble next to it reaches the rest of the staff directly.
Tier 2 · Daily Use

My Day — your task list Start here

My Day is the card at the top of Home, and it is the thing to read first each morning. The system assigns work to you here — you do not have to go looking for it.

Four chips run across the top: Overdue, Today, This wk and Done. Underneath, Things to do today is your list, and Updates since you were last here shows what the rest of the team has been doing.

Tick the round checkbox to close a task. Click anywhere else on the row to open the interaction behind it.

Most tasks carry a button that takes you straight to the right screen:

ButtonWhat it is for
Take AttendanceAn event finished. Opens the signup list already in attendance mode.
Open Event SummaryThe pink-sheet numbers are still blank for that session.
Send Recording & NotesSend the recording and slides to everyone who signed up.
Compare the two recordsTwo contacts look like the same person. See Contacts.
Found the payment — mark paidA membership was started but never paid.
Open ContactA returning family needs their registration checked.
Cancel & archive pendingPending signups on an event happening today.
Why some tasks have no button. Send Zoom Link and Send Slides are reminders to you, not automated actions. Send the email yourself, then tick the box. Nothing checks that you sent it, so if you tick without sending, the family gets nothing.
The list stops at 15. If you have more than fifteen open tasks, the count and the list both show fifteen. Anything with a follow-up date more than two weeks out is hidden entirely. Use View all my tasks to see the rest.

The Home Page

Above My Day sit five counters: Active Events / Programs, Interactions logged this week, Follow-ups due in the next seven days, Needs review, and Closed. They are a pulse check, not a to-do list — your work is in My Day.

Down the right side are the Community Calendar, which puts a dot on every day something is running, and Recent signups, a live list of the last thirty people to sign up for anything. Click any signup to jump to that event.

At the bottom is a collapsed drawer, Yearly audit & reports, holding the PTI / OSEP Audit Export. It is folded away because it is used once or twice a year, not because it is unimportant — see Reports.

On an iPad in landscape. The right-hand column is hidden to make room. Turn the iPad upright and the calendar and recent signups come back.

Alerts Bell

The Alerts bell in the top bar collects three things: Change Requests waiting on you, your follow-ups, and website alerts such as a new volunteer application or partner request.

It refreshes every couple of minutes rather than instantly, so a brand-new item can take a moment to appear.

Change Request approvals only arrive here. When someone approves your request to edit a locked record, the bell is the only place you will see it, and the notice itself is the link that unlocks the edit. Clear the bell without reading it and you will have to ask again.

Presence & Chat

The coloured dots by each name at the top show who is signed in right now. Green is online.

The chat bubble at the bottom left opens staff chat. Pick a person, type, send.

Request Screen Sharing asks the other person to show you their screen. It posts a message asking them to click Share My Screen — nothing happens until they do. Nobody needs to install anything.

If screen sharing will not connect. It links the two computers directly, which some office networks block. The dot stays amber until a picture actually arrives. If it never turns green, fall back to a phone call.

Daily Session Sheet

An email goes out at 6 AM Hawaii time, every day, to the staff on the recipient list. It covers every active event and programme with its confirmed and pending counts, what changed in the last 24 hours, interactions overdue or due today, and any website submissions waiting for a reply.

Who receives it is managed in Admin → List Management → Daily Report Recipients, where there is also a Preview Today's Report button.

Logging Interactions

An Interaction is one logged conversation with a contact: a phone call, an email, an in-person visit, anything. Logging interactions builds the family's history so when a colleague picks up the phone next month they can see what has already happened.

  1. Click + New Interaction in the top bar.
  2. Start typing the contact's name. If nobody matches, a + appears so you can add them without losing what you have typed.
  3. Pick the channel and type, write your notes, set a follow-up date if one is needed.
  4. Set the status — Open, Needs review or Closed.
  5. Save.
There are no drafts. Every save is final and visible to the team. Write the note you would want a colleague to find. If you are not ready, leave the interaction Open and add to it later.

Two checkboxes on the form do more than they look:

  • Materials distributed records how many of each item you handed out. Those numbers feed the grant reports, so they matter.
  • Case Advocacy hands the family to an advocate, creates that advocate's own follow-up three months out, and keeps the family's Connect-Gen documents from being auto-deleted.

Website Submissions

Anything the public sends in lands under CMS → Website Data, in tabs: Provider Requests, Anti-Bullying Pledges, Contact Messages and Calendar Requests. Each has its own status chain, and each moves one step at a time so two people are not working the same item.

Viewing Event Signups

From CMS → Events & Programs, click View Signups on any event card. The full detail is in Signups & Sessions; the four buttons across the top are:

Take AttendanceSwitch the list into attendance mode.
Session SheetA printable roster for the day.
Export CSVThe list as a spreadsheet.
SummaryThe Event Summary form — the pink-sheet numbers.

Marking Attendance

  1. Open View Signups and click Take Attendance.
  2. Tick everyone who came. Mark All Present does the whole session at once.
  3. Click Save Attendance.
Anything left unticked is recorded as a no-show. That is deliberate — it is how the no-show numbers get into the grant reports. So only save once the room is settled, and if someone arrives late, tick them and save again.

Saving attendance on a one-time event creates two follow-up tasks automatically: send the Zoom link, and send the slides. They appear in the right person's My Day.

Searching

The box at the top searches signups, interactions, contacts, grants and partner resources at once. It matches phone numbers in any format, so 8085551234 finds (808) 555-1234. Click a result to open that exact record.

Tier 3 · Full Reference

Contacts

The list of everyone LDAH works with. Filter by letter, by Members only or by Advocacy only, and export what you are looking at with Export CSV.

The count in the heading ignores your filters. Contacts (492) is the whole book, not the twelve you have filtered to. The export, by contrast, does respect the filters.

The contact card

Click a name to open the card. The left rail holds collapsible sections — Demographics & address, Membership, Materials received, and Screening. Opening one closes the others.

The main column shows the children on file, the latest note, and the activity timeline: every signup, interaction and email in order. Show all notes gathers every note ever written about this family into one list.

Second parent and children

A contact can carry a second parent or guardian, and any number of children. Both parents then receive every family email.

The second parent's email is the key. A name with no email is not saved. If you clear the email on an existing record, the whole second-parent block goes with it — name included.

Duplicate contacts

Each morning the system looks for contacts sharing a phone number and raises a task for whoever handles data review. The task gives you both records side by side and two buttons:

  • Same person — merge them moves every interaction, signup, screening and child onto the record you keep, and keeps the second email address.
  • Two different people — keep both changes nothing and is remembered permanently, so the pair is never raised again.
Decide with a button, not the checkbox. Merging cannot be undone from the dashboard. And if you close the task with the My Day checkbox without pressing either button, the pair is left undecided and will not come back — so nobody will ever look at it again. Two staff sharing an office line is a normal reason to keep both.

Screening & Consent

Screening runs on consent first, results second. The Screening section on the contact card holds both.

Consent

Before a child can be screened we need a signed consent. It arrives one of three ways: the parent signs the online form, they sign at the kiosk on the day, or they sign a paper form.

For a paper form, open the contact, open Screening, and click Upload consent form. A scan or a clear photo is fine — PDF, JPG, PNG or HEIC, up to 25 MB. It then appears there with a View button that opens it without leaving the page.

Every contact has this section, whether or not they have ever been screened. If there is nothing on file, the upload button is the only thing inside.

These forms are handled like the Connect-Gen documents. They are not reachable from the public internet, only signed-in staff can open one, and each viewing is recorded in the Audit Log. That is deliberate — a signed consent names a child and carries a parent's signature.

Where a consent exists, the card shows the wording the parent agreed to, the health questionnaire, and how it was signed — either electronically with the date and address it came from, or Paper consent — signed on the day, filed in the office. The distinction matters: it would be untrue to describe a handwritten signature as electronic on a child's health record.

"Awaiting parent" means the consent, not the results. A contact showing Screening requested is waiting for the form to come back, even if the screening itself has already happened on paper.

Results

Once consent is on file, Enter results opens the official results form — hearing (otoscopy, OAE, tympanometry, each ear) and vision (distance each eye, Lea Symbols, PlusOptix), plus date, site and who screened. Afterwards the button reads Edit / send results.

Save & email family sends a real email immediately. It sits one button away from a plain Save results. Save first, read it back, then send. If the contact has no email address the send button is disabled.

Members & Membership

Members pay for a membership on the public site and get a login to the Member Portal. A gold Member pill appears on their contact card with the level, and (pending) until the payment is confirmed.

When someone starts a membership and never pays, a task appears with two buttons: Resend payment request, and Found the payment — mark paid.

Only mark paid if you have seen the money in PayPal. Nothing checks PayPal for you. Marking paid sends the thank-you email carrying their portal login and switches the membership on.

Reports → Membership Reports holds the totals, the pending list, paid members, CSV exports and a box to paste a PayPal export into for comparison.

That report hides things by default. Test records, staff records and removed rows are left out until you tick Show removed and Show test / staff records. Without that, abandoned checkout attempts once made the total read far higher than the money actually received.

Interactions

The full log, filterable by channel, type, owner and status. Statuses are Open, Needs review and Closed.

StatusMeaning
OpenStill live — something is outstanding.
Needs reviewYou want another pair of eyes before it closes.
ClosedFinished. Still searchable, still on the family's history.

You can hand an interaction to someone else with the owner picker; it moves into their My Day and the change is recorded.

Events & Programs

One list holds everything — workshops, ongoing programmes, screenings, booths and flyers. The chips across the top filter it: All, Learning Labs, Parent Talk Cafe, Connect-Gen, Screening, Remote Signup, Outreach Booth, Flyer.

Cards are grouped Active/Upcoming, then Past, then Expired and Archived behind the Show expired & archived toggle.

Adding an event

+ Add Event opens a three-step funnel for every type:

  1. Drop the flyer in — JPG, PNG, WebP or PDF up to 6 MB. It reads the flyer and fills in the title, description, location, dates and times, and guesses the type. No flyer? Use skip and pick the type.
  2. Confirm or change the type. The guess is outlined and labelled.
  3. Open editor hands you the right form, already filled in.
The funnel saves nothing. It only fills the form in. Read every field — especially the year, which is the thing the flyer reader most often gets wrong — then press Save. If you save a new event with a date in the past, it will ask whether you meant to.

The three lifecycle dates

Start ShowingWhen it appears on the public site.
Move to PastWhen it drops into the Past section.
RemoveWhen it disappears from the site altogether.

On each card

Depending on type you will see counts, per-date chips, a pending chip that opens the signup list, an Announce button, a QR button, Edit and Archive. Amber means something needs attention; red means full.

Signups & Sessions

View Signups opens the list for an event. A strip along the top summarises it — for Connect-Gen that is families, awaiting worksheet, ready, and the next session; for a Remote Signup it is people against the venue capacity.

Each row shows the person, their status, the child if there is one, and pills telling you where they came from — via pop-up, via homepage, via announcement, via registration email — plus Returning for a family we have seen before.

The … at the end of a row holds the less common actions: the registration detail, notes, changing the dates they are booked on, resending their confirmation, and archiving.

Recurring programmes

Sessions are grouped by date. Upcoming ones are navy, past ones amber with a PAST chip, cancelled ones red and struck through. Dates with nobody on them fold into a single line you can expand.

Duplicate signups

A red Duplicate pill means the same person appears twice on overlapping dates. Opening it recommends which to keep.

Resolving a duplicate signup deletes the extra record outright. No email is sent, and any attendance or feedback on the removed one goes with it. Check which copy holds the history before you choose.

Cancelling a Session

Each upcoming date has a checkbox. What happens next depends on who is booked:

Nobody signed upA short confirm, then it is cancelled.
People are signed upThe Cancel & Reschedule window opens first.
Restoring, or a past dateHappens immediately.

In the Cancel & Reschedule window every family gets a dropdown: move them to another session, or cancel and email them. Bulk Apply does the whole list at once. The confirm button stays disabled until every family has been dealt with, and it tells you how many emails it is about to send.

Nothing is sent until you confirm. Closing with Back puts the checkbox back so the screen does not claim something that did not happen.

To cancel dates further out — a holiday, a vacation — use Future Dates, which lists every session from beyond the next month through the end of the year.

Connect-Gen

Connect-Gen is the ongoing one-to-one programme, and it has more moving parts than anything else.

It runs on four schedules: Oahu weekly, Hilo on the 2nd Thursday and Kona on the 3rd, which are named separately because they are an hour apart on the same island. A family books one session, not a term.

The Parent Report Worksheet

After registering, the family is emailed a worksheet. Until it comes back the signup shows Awaiting Worksheet rather than Confirmed.

Reminders go out every three days, up to three times. Beside the Awaiting Worksheet pill is a Resend button for a manual nudge; it shows how many times it has been used.

Resend emails the family straight away, and it is not subject to the three-reminder cap. After two ignored reminders the system creates a phone-call task for that session's presenter instead.

Staff can open and edit the worksheet from either the signup or the contact card. In practice the advocate fills in columns D and E after speaking with the family.

Documents

Families upload their IEP and evaluation before the session. Connect-Gen Documents shows them side by side with a full-screen preview, and three closing actions: No Additional Support, Case Advocacy, or destroy them now.

Every open and every preview is recorded. These are a child's confidential records. The documents are deleted automatically after the session unless the family is taken on for case advocacy.
Thursday families are never sent a consent form. Oahu, Hilo and Kona Thursday families sign on arrival, so their pill correctly says awaiting worksheet, never awaiting consent.

Event Summary

Opened with the Summary button. The form is colour-banded: blue is who came, green is what they said, and pink is what only you can fill in.

The pink band carries the numbers the audit and grant reports are built from. Each row shows the system's own count as a hint — type over it only if you know better, and the hint will note that you overrode it.

It also holds Walk-in / Manual Attendees for people who turned up without signing up, Training Materials with how many were packed and handed out, the tier of support, and the presenter's comments.

A walk-in with no email is counted but not kept. They add to the attendance total, but no contact record is created, so there is nobody to follow up with. Add an email if you want them in Contacts.
If it is left blank it is simply lost. Nothing else in the system captures these numbers.

Events Dashboard Admin

A month at a glance, under the Admin tab. Every dated session appears in its day, with a dropdown to set or change the presenter.

A teal edge means a presenter is assigned; amber means nobody is, so an unstaffed week is obvious at a glance. Use the arrows to change month, or Today to come back.

The names come from List Management → Presenters. Assigning a presenter here never disturbs anything already recorded on the Event Summary.

A presenter's name must match their staff account exactly. "Chassidy" where the account says "Chassidy Kruse" will show on the dashboard but tasks for those sessions will never reach her.

Recordings & Slides

After a session, send the recording and any handouts to everyone who signed up. Open it from the event card, the Event Summary, or the task in My Day.

The Zoom link is required; files are optional and you can attach several. People marked as no-shows get a different, gentler message.

Why the link is required. Nineteen Learning Lab emails once went out with an empty link. The form will not send without one now.

Files are capped at 10 MB each and 20 MB in total. Links stay good for two weeks and the files are cleaned up after fifteen days.

Recordings Archive Admin

This is what paid members see in the Member Portal — a rolling twelve months of session recordings. Each card shows whether it is On the portal or Hidden. Edit a title, description, cover image or attached slides; Remove hides it from members and Restore puts it back.

Zoom Platform Recordings, below it, lists the raw copies Zoom keeps for about ten days. Each has a one-click link you can paste into an email, and Publish to member archive for one that was never sent.

Remove is reversible; editing is not. Removing only hides a recording. But replacing a cover image or removing an attached file deletes the old one for good, and editing a summary changes what members read straight away with no confirmation.

Announcement Blasts SuperAdmin

Announce on an event card emails people who have not yet signed up. On a multi-date event you choose which date you are filling.

  1. Pick the date, and whether it goes to all contacts or parents only.
  2. Review the roster. Quick links: select all, none, Big Island only, or not yet sent.
  3. Send yourself a test.
  4. Type SEND to unlock the button, then send.
This is the most dangerous button in the dashboard. It is real mass email and there is no undo. Anyone already emailed about this date is never pre-ticked. Send the test first — every time.
Big Island only really means the Big Island. It matches by postcode, and it deliberately leaves out Kilauea — the town of that name is on Kaua‘i, and two families there were nearly sent a Hilo invitation.

For entering a historical pink sheet by hand — name, date, presenter, location, tier, materials and who attended. Everyone added is marked as having attended, and the event is filed so it never appears in the live Events list.

PTI / OSEP Audit Export

In the Yearly audit & reports drawer on Home. Produces a spreadsheet with one tab per grant year, running March to February.

Its totals will not match the on-screen report unless that report's Program filter is set to All including One-Offs. The export always includes everything.

CMS Dashboard

The front page of the CMS tab. Four counters across the top — pledges, volunteer applications, event signups, provider requests — each clickable through to the list behind it.

Below them, Signups by Event shows two charts side by side: upcoming on the left, past on the right, one bar per session, coloured by event type. On the past side the solid part of each bar is how many actually attended, so the pale gap is the no-show rate.

Then Content Overview — how many team members, resources, events, FAQs and videos are live — the Resource Update Cycle progress bar, and Quick Actions.

The chart and the signups counter differ on purpose. The chart leaves out cancelled signups and archived events, and counts a family booked on two sessions once for each. It answers "how full is each session", not "how many people are there".

Team & Board

The staff and board listings on the public site. Two tabs, same shape: add a person, upload a photo, write the biography, drag to reorder. Archiving takes someone off the website without deleting the record.

Galleries

Two photo galleries feed the site: Gallery 1 is Who We Are, Gallery 2 is Volunteer. Upload, caption, reorder, archive.

Photo tips. Landscape photos sit better in the layout than portrait. Keep files under a few megabytes so the page stays quick on a phone.

Community Resources

The partner directory on the public site. Each entry carries the organisation, what they do, who to contact and where they serve.

The update cycle

Twice a year every partner is asked to confirm their details are current — spring and autumn. The indicator beside the list shows when the next nudge goes out, or that the cycle has ended.

A green dashed edge means confirmed recently. Amber with an Update Pending pill means a partner has sent changes: Review Update shows current against proposed with the differences picked out, and you can edit before approving.

Once the cycle closes, nudges stop. Partners who never replied become phone-call tasks instead, because an email that arrives after the deadline helps nobody.
Send Update Request issues a fresh link and kills the old one. If a partner is midway through the form, sending again will break what they had.

Partner Applications, above the list, holds organisations asking to be added.

FAQs

The questions and answers on the public site. Add, edit, reorder, archive. Group them by category so the page stays readable.

Volunteers

Two tabs: Opportunities, which is what appears on the Volunteer page, and Applications, which is who applied.

Applications move New → Contacted → Interviewing → Accepted or Declined. Export them all as a spreadsheet grouped by opportunity.

Always Post keeps an opportunity visible regardless of its start and end dates.

Website Data

Everything the public sends in, in four tabs — Provider Requests, Anti-Bullying Pledges, Contact Messages and Calendar Requests. Each has its own status chain, and pledges show a green tick once the automatic acknowledgement has gone out.

Page Editor

Edit the wording on public pages directly. Pick a page on the left, edit in the middle, save.

This is the live website. There is no preview step and no draft. Save and it is public.

Downloads

The file library — PDFs, Word documents, spreadsheets, slide decks. Up to 25 MB per file. This is where you find something to send a family.

Downloads and Resources are different things. Downloads is the file cabinet and everyone can see it. Resources in the CMS tab is the partner organisation directory on the public site.

List Management Admin

The options behind the dropdowns, plus the settings the dashboard runs on. Fifteen cards in five groups, all collapsed until you open them. The search box looks inside every list at once.

GroupCards
Interaction form optionsChannels, Interaction Types, Grants / Programs, Titles, Departments
Resources & materialsResource Types, Materials
People & recipientsPresenters, Daily Report Recipients, Team Personas
Meeting linksProgram Zoom, Event Zoom
Dashboard & website contentSidebar Tips, Donate Block × 2
Changes apply immediately, for everyone. There are no confirmations anywhere on this screen.

Presenters feeds three places: the Event Summary form, the Events Dashboard, and the presenter field you must fill in before an interaction can be closed. Archiving a name keeps old summaries readable while removing it from new dropdowns.

Program Zoom is for Connect-Gen and other ongoing programmes; Event Zoom is for Learning Labs and one-off events. Which one an event uses is set by its Source field, not by what kind of event it is. Parent Talk Cafe uses neither.

Team Personas sets the names, titles, phone numbers and signatures inside emails — not the From address, which is fixed. Donate Block sets the donate link used in most family emails, on the website and in the app.

Retire, do not rename. For Materials and Presenters, archive the old entry and add a new one rather than renaming mid-year, so last quarter's counts still line up with the name they were recorded under. Personas and Donate Block reach emails within about five minutes, or immediately if you press Refresh Now.

Email Log Admin

Every email the system has sent, newest first, with counts for today, the last seven days, failures, and how long since the last send. Filter by type, search by recipient or subject, and click any row to see exactly what was sent.

Resend sends immediately. One click, no confirmation, and the family gets it again.

Reminder Schedule Admin

A forecast of the reminder emails due to go out. It sends nothing itself.

3 days beforeGoes out at 4 PM Hawaii time.
Day of — virtualAbout 30 minutes before the session starts.
Day of — in personThat morning at 8 AM, because families have to travel.

Feedback follow-ups are listed below the reminders. Anything already sent is in the Email Log.

Audit Log Admin

A record of the administrative changes the system tracks — who changed what, and when. Search by action, person or role. It shows the most recent thousand entries and cannot be edited or cleared, which is rather the point.

Emergency Contacts & Chat Logs Admin

Emergency Contacts is the quick-reference list for urgent situations. Chat Logs is the archive of staff chat, for when you need to find what was agreed.

User Roles SuperAdmin

Who can see what. Seven roles, from superAdmin down to partner. Each person also has checkboxes controlling which alert categories reach their bell.

Follow-up counts always show. Your own overdue work is added to your bell whatever your alert settings say — you cannot switch off your own to-do list.

Change Requests

Some records lock after saving. To edit one, request the change; an admin approves it and the approval arrives in your Alerts bell, where the notice itself unlocks the edit. It is a one-time unlock, so make the change while you are there.

Training Videos

Short screen recordings covering the dashboard. Search rather than browse — press / to jump into the search box — and the category chips show how many videos each holds.

If a video will not play, its sharing setting is wrong. It must be shared as "anyone with the link" or nobody outside the owner's account can see it.
