Aloha — the contact card, end to end

Six hundred and sixteen people, and one card each. Reading first, editing second — nothing you do in the first half changes a thing.

Part 1 · The list

Contacts, in the left menu

The count in brackets is live. If somebody adds a family this afternoon, that number goes up.

Part 1 · The list

Four ways to find one person

The search box looks at name, email, phone, type and organisation all at once. Two or three letters is usually enough.

Part 1 · The list

What a row already tells you

A green tick under OPTED IN means they have agreed to receive our announcements. No tick means they have not, and the system will not email them.

Part 2 · Reading a card

The whole card, in one look

Left: who they are. Right: what has happened. Bottom: every email we sent them. Nothing on this screen can be changed by accident.

Part 2 · Left column

Who they are, and how to reach them

The badge colour is a quick tell. Professional means somebody we work alongside — not a family we serve.

Part 2 · Left column

Organisation, and which programme

If this is blank or wrong, the person's work does not land in the right funding bucket. It is worth a look every time you open a card.

Part 2 · Left column

Event announcements — their consent

Opted in = we may email them Not opted in = we may not Only they can change their mind
Part 2 · Left column

Two folded sections, waiting for a click

A triangle pointing right means folded. Pointing down means open. Nothing is hidden from you — it is just tidied away.

Part 2 · Left column

The mailing address, and the screening status

Nothing on file is a normal, healthy state. It is not an error and it is not something you need to fix.

Part 2 · Right column

The child block, and the latest note

The note shows who wrote it, when, and which interaction it came from. Show all 3 notes underneath opens the rest.

Part 2 · Right column

All the notes, newest first

Part 2 · Right column

The activity timeline

Part 3 · The emails

The unsubscribe line at the foot

This is a legal requirement on every announcement, and it links straight back to their consent setting on the card.

Part 3 · The emails

“I never got that email”

Did it go?

If the row is there with a green Sent, it left our system. If there is no row, it never went — usually because they are not opted in.

When did it go?

The date and time are right there. You can tell them exactly which day to look for in their inbox.

What did it say?

Open the row and read them the flyer over the phone, or the sign-up link underneath it.

No blaming anybody, no guessing. The card already knows.

Part 4 · Editing

Edit Contact opens the same person as a form

From here on you are changing the record. Cancel undoes everything — nothing is saved until you press Save Contact at the bottom.

Part 4 · Editing

Name, and what kind of contact they are

A star means the system will not let you save without it. Type changes how they are counted in the reports, so it is worth getting right.

Part 4 · Editing

The second parent has to be filled in to be emailed

Second parent left blank means second parent never gets an announcement. Ask for it, and put it in.

Part 4 · Editing

External advocate — search and pick

A translator or a community advocate goes here. Clear on the right removes whoever is set, if things change.

Part 4 · Editing

The consent checkbox, and what it does not stop

Ticked — they hear about events Unticked — no marketing blasts

Confirmations and reminders keep working either way. Only tick or untick this if the person asked you to.

Part 4 · Editing

Where they belong, and one line about them

Write the note for a colleague who has never met this person. One good sentence beats a blank box.

Part 4 · Editing

Military status

If you do not know, leave it as it is and ask the family next time you speak. A guess here is worse than a blank.

Part 4 · Editing

Child information

Age range and ethnicity are dropdowns, so you cannot mistype them. Fill what you know and leave the rest for next time.

Part 4 · Editing

Disability categories, and more than one child

Tick every one that applies — Suspected is there for a reason, and so is No IDEA Disability. Never overwrite one child with another.

Part 4 · Editing

Training and outreach

Ask it as a real question when you meet somebody. It is the only way we learn what works.

Part 4 · Editing

Accommodations, then Save Contact

An interpreter, an accessible room, large print. Nothing is saved until you press Save Contact.

Part 5 · The paperwork

Where the forms live

Sign in sheets, request for assistance, evaluations, the workshop recap, the budget template. Always take the copy from here — it is the current version.
